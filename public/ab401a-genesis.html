AB401A — Interpret & Apply the Book of Genesis Today

This room reconstructs the Bible-study subject you actually completed. It keeps the course's pattern: read carefully, understand literary and cultural context, compare interpretations, identify theology, then ask what faithful application follows.

How this differs from a generic Genesis study

The content follows your retained AB401A notes and official assessment design: the Old Testament grand narrative, Pentateuch/source questions, Genesis 1–2 literary form, Genesis 1–11 exegesis, creation/science positions, universal/local Flood readings, ancient Near Eastern comparisons, Abrahamic covenant, Jacob/Joseph, and the six official theological themes.

Historical-course-content note: the creation/science section preserves what the 2013 class surveyed. Scientific claims from those notes are not silently upgraded into present-day consensus.
Original assessments

Can you still do the actual work?

20% · Old Testament Timeline

Recreate Genesis–Malachi as a visual timeline with major eras/dates, key story points and significant theological themes.

60% · Genesis 1–11 Exegesis

Choose Creation, Fall, Cain & Abel, Genealogies, Flood or Babel. Use grammatico-historical exegesis; discuss literary/cultural context and differing Christian standpoints; identify at least two theological themes; formulate specific contemporary implications/application.

20% · Patriarch Reflection

Choose Abraham, Jacob or Joseph. What did they learn about God? How does their story advance salvation/redemption? What concrete application follows for life/ministry?
