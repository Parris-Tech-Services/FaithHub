# How to pray to Jesus: the saints and their teaching (31 October 2026)

## Scope and methods
This is a Christian, ecumenically readable learning resource, not a claim that all traditions agree on every doctrine. The user-facing guide encourages direct prayer to Jesus, and prayer to the Father through the Son in the Spirit. It does **not** require praying to or through departed saints. Sources below are primary texts wherever possible; podcast links are publisher/episode pages. Each summary is an original paraphrase rather than a supposed verbatim quote.

## Scripture first
- [Matthew 6:5–13](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/MAT.6.5-13.NIV): prayer without performance or excessive words; Jesus' model prayer.
- [Luke 11:1–13](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/LUK.11.1-13.NIV): Jesus teaches his disciples, including asking, seeking and knocking.
- [Matthew 11:28–30](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/MAT.11.28-30.NIV): Jesus invites the weary.
- [John 15:4–5](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.15.4-5.NIV): abiding in Christ.
- [Ephesians 2:18](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/EPH.2.18.NIV): through Christ access to the Father by one Spirit.
- [Philippians 4:6–7](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/PHP.4.6-7.NIV): requests with thanksgiving.
- [Romans 8:26](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/ROM.8.26.NIV): the Spirit helps in our weakness.

## Primary historical witnesses and their actual emphasis

1. **Augustine of Hippo, Letter 130 to Proba (c. 412)**: sections 17–20 on prayer as enlarged desire, brief prayers from Egyptian Christians, and wordless tears. [Full letter, New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1102130.htm). Not “if you say enough words, God will give you the answer you want.”
2. **Teresa of Ávila, The Way of Perfection, chapter 26**: take Jesus as your companion when beginning prayer; approach the Lord personally and remain with him. [CCEL chapter](https://ccel.org/ccel/teresa/way/way.i.xxxii.html). The related “close sharing between friends” formulation is attributed to Teresa in [Catechism §2709](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_four/section_one/chapter_three/article_1/iii_contemplative_prayer.index.html), but the on-site summary is based on chapter 26.
3. **Ignatius of Loyola, Spiritual Exercises §§53–54**: a colloquy is conversation like a friend with a friend; bring your affairs and petitions to Christ. [Full text](https://www.orderofthecommonlife.org/spiritual-exercises-full-text). Do not imply every thought or mental picture is a revelation.
4. **Brother Lawrence, Practice of the Presence of God, Fourth Conversation and Eighth Letter**: converse with God while working; gently return when thoughts wander without anxiety or self-reproach. [CCEL text](https://ccel.org/ccel/lawrence/practice.all.html).
5. **Benedict of Nursia, Rule chapter 20**: prayer with humility and purity rather than sheer length; short sincere communal prayer is usual. [Project Gutenberg full rule](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/50040/50040-h/50040-h.htm).
6. **Thérèse of Lisieux, Story of a Soul, chapter 11**: spontaneous, childlike heartfelt prayer in gratitude and sorrow without searching for elaborate formulas. [CCEL text](https://ccel.org/ccel/therese/autobio.all.html).
7. **The historic Jesus Prayer**: “Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner” is used in Orthodox prayer of the heart; a simple, accessible personal invocation may be used without undertaking specialised contemplative or breathing methods. [Orthodox Church in America](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/the-jesus-prayer). The OCA cautions against independently pursuing advanced hesychast technique.

## Editorial safeguards
- Historical quotations were deliberately **not** invented. The guide's example prayers are original FaithHub text, not a formula guaranteeing answers.
- Prayer should not replace safeguarding, medical or mental-health care, practical help, Scripture, the local church, or wise counsel.
- The breathing/posture instruction is ordinary grounding, not a prescription for intensive hesychast practice.
- No private notes, analytics, tracking or hosted third-party audio in this feature. All audio links open at the publisher.
- Site uses the existing accessible FaithHub CSS, native elements, strong focus rings and responsive cards; no new JavaScript or backend.
