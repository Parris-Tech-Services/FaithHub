Four ways of talking with God

Bring your thanks, your pain, your needs, and the needs of others to a Father who welcomes honest prayer.

A helpful distinction: These are four complementary forms of “talking with God”, adapted for daily use. John Mark Comer's Prayer Practice has four broader movements: talking to God, talking with God, listening to God, and being with God. The original teaching groups petition and intercession together. There is no required order, quota or streak.

Choose whichever is true for you today. You can pray in a sentence, linger, or simply read a Psalm. These prompts and sample prayers are original, informed by Scripture and Practicing the Way, not quotations from Comer.

01 · Receive what is good

Thankfulness

Tell God about a real gift, ordinary beauty, provision or kindness. Gratitude is a response to God's generosity, not a demand to feel cheerful.

Read: 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18 · James 1:17

Ask yourself

What small good thing was here today? Can I notice it without denying what was difficult?

Example prayer: Father, thank you for this gift. Help me receive it and be generous with what you've given me.

02 · Bring what hurts

Lament

Bring grief, frustration, anger or an unanswered prayer to God. You don't have to tidy up your feelings or finish with a happy ending.

Read: Psalm 13 · Psalm 88

Ask yourself

What feels wrong or heavy? What do I wish God knew—even if I don't have an answer?

Example prayer: Lord, I am hurting and confused. Hear me in this place. Give me room to be honest with you.

03 · Ask for help

Petition

Ask the Father plainly for what you need: wisdom, provision, healing, courage, guidance, forgiveness or the Holy Spirit. You may keep asking without trying to control the outcome.

Read: Luke 11:5–13 · Philippians 4:6–7

Ask yourself

What do I actually need today? What can I ask for directly, and what responsible step might be mine to take?

Example prayer: Father, please help me with this need. Give me your Spirit, wisdom and courage for the next faithful step.

04 · Ask for others

Intercession

Pray for someone's wellbeing, for communities and for the world. Hold their dignity and agency in mind, and look for a practical expression of love when appropriate.

Read: 1 Timothy 2:1–2 · James 5:16

Ask yourself

Who needs peace, provision, healing, justice or encouragement? What kind action could I take without overstepping?

Example prayer: Lord, please be near to those who need your care. Show me how to love well and help where I can.

Practice gently

Begin where you are. Thankfulness can coexist with pain; lament need not end in instant optimism. Petition and intercession are not guarantees of a specific result. Prayer doesn't replace appropriate practical help, healthcare or safeguarding.
